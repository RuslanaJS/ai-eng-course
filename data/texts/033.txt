Residential developers sold 12,500 homes across Poland’s seven largest housing markets in the third quarter of 2026, up 5.7 pct from the previous quarter. They launched 12,800 new units, while the number of homes available for sale reached 70,200 at the end of September. According to real estate consultancy JLL, sales and new supply are broadly balanced, but the growing stock of completed unsold homes and shifting buyer sentiment point to a more uncertain outlook.

Developers sold around 4,400 homes in September, including 1,400 in Warsaw. The capital recorded the highest quarterly sales, at 3,900 units. Kraków, the Tri-City and Wrocław also posted solid results.
After the first nine months of the year, sales across the seven markets totalled 37,200 homes, putting a full-year result of around 50,000 transactions within reach. Achieving that figure would require sales of approximately 12,800 units in the fourth quarter.
2026 has brought a more balanced market after two years in which new supply significantly outpaced sales. However, conditions vary considerably by location. Supply is broadly in line with demand in Warsaw and the Tri-City, while at the current sales pace it would take more than two years to sell all available homes in Łódź and almost three years in Katowice.
Aleksandra Gawrońska, director of residential market research at JLL
Completed unsold homes account for a growing share of supply
Completed homes are becoming an increasingly significant part of the market. At the end of September, they accounted for 25 pct of available stock, compared with 16 pct a year earlier. The number of completed unsold units exceeded 17,000, having nearly doubled over the year.
The highest shares of completed homes were recorded in Katowice, at 38 pct, followed by Łódź at 33 pct and Poznań at 31 pct. In Kraków, the figure stood at 29 pct, while Warsaw and Wrocław were both at around 20 pct. The Tri-City remained the exception, with completed homes accounting for slightly more than 10 pct of available stock.
Asking prices remain above transaction prices
Asking prices were broadly stable during the quarter, although annual increases varied across the country. Warsaw recorded the strongest growth, at 6.8 pct, followed by Poznań at 6 pct and Wrocław at 5.2 pct. Prices rose by 4.1 pct in the Tri-City, 2.9 pct in Łódź and 1.7 pct in Kraków. Katowice was the only market to record an annual decline, at 0.4 pct.
Asking prices also remain above the prices buyers are actually paying. In Warsaw, the average asking price was close to PLN 20,000 per sqm, compared with approximately PLN 18,400 per sqm for homes sold. The gap was also pronounced in the Tri-City, at PLN 18,400 versus PLN 16,200 per sqm, and in Katowice, at PLN 12,700 versus PLN 11,300 per sqm. In Warsaw and the Tri-City, the difference partly reflects the higher proportion of premium properties in the available supply, while sales are concentrated more heavily in the mainstream segment.
Buyer sentiment and mortgage costs cloud the outlook
Buyer optimism, lower mortgage rates and improving affordability supported demand in the first half of 2026. In the third quarter, concerns about possible interest rate increases and higher construction costs may have encouraged some buyers to purchase sooner than planned. Consumer sentiment has remained subdued for several quarters, while the mortgage market is gradually returning to normal following a period of widespread refinancing.
Fears of higher interest rates and rising construction costs may be supporting demand for now, but these same factors could weigh on the market in the coming quarters. Year-on-year sales growth in 2027 is unlikely. The outlook for 2028 and beyond will depend on inflation and buyer sentiment.
Kazimierz Kirejczyk, senior strategy advisor for the housing sector at JLL
Higher fuel, energy and building material costs, together with legislative changes, are expected to put further pressure on development costs and margins. With limited scope to raise selling prices, developers may find it increasingly difficult to pass these increases on to buyers.
